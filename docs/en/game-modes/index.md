# Game Modes

Mordhau's modes fall into four rough groups: objective modes where teams fight
over ground, team modes where the only objective is the other team, modes where
everyone is on their own, and one where the enemy is the game itself.

Which ones you see in the server browser depends on what people are hosting at
the time. The large objective modes carry most of the population, and the
smaller ones live mostly on community servers.

::: warning
The lineup shifts with patches, and community servers frequently run modified
rules. Treat this page as what each mode is for, not as a promise about what is
in the browser tonight.
:::

## Objective modes

### Frontline

The mode most people mean when they talk about Mordhau. Two teams push a line
of capture points across a map, and taking one moves the front and the spawns
with it. Deaths are cheap because respawns come in waves, so the shape of a
Frontline match is a shifting mass of bodies rather than a series of duels.

It is also the loudest, most chaotic thing in the game and the worst place to
learn the combat. You will die to people you never saw. That is not you playing
badly, that is the mode.

### Invasion

Asymmetric. One team attacks a sequence of objectives in order and the other
holds them, with a timer running. Attackers have to finish the whole chain to
win; defenders only have to stall.

Longer and more structured than Frontline, and objectives tend to funnel the
fighting into places where the map itself matters.

## Team modes

### Team deathmatch

Two teams, a score limit, and no objective beyond the other team. Respawns are
quick. `TDM_` on a server map list.

### Skirmish

Round based, and there are no respawns. Die and you sit the round out, which
changes how people play immediately — nobody trades a life for a kill when it
is their only one. Rounds are short and the pace is closer to a series of small
engagements than to Frontline's scrum. `SKM_` on a server map list.

## Everyone for themselves

### Free for all

No teams, no objectives. The best place in the game to actually practise
fighting, because you get a high number of engagements per minute and nobody is
relying on you. `FFA_` on a server map list.

Expect to be attacked while already fighting someone else. Learning to bail out
of a fight you are winning is a real skill here.

### Battle Royale

Last player standing, on a shrinking play area, starting with whatever you find
rather than a loadout you built.

## Against the game

### Horde

Co-operative. A team holds out against waves of AI that get harder as the match
goes on, with gold between waves to spend on better equipment. Forgiving, and a
reasonable way to get familiar with a weapon without a real opponent punishing
every mistake.

## Duels

Not a mode in the menu so much as a convention. Duel servers are ordinary
servers set up for one-on-one fights, with etiquette enforced by the people on
them: you wait your turn, you bow, and you do not interrupt a fight in
progress. See the [glossary](/en/glossary/) if that convention is
new to you.

This is where people go to actually get better at the combat system, and it is
where the mechanics on the [combat mechanics](/en/combat-mechanics/) page are
worth studying properly.

## Where to start

If you have just installed the game, the order that wastes the least of your
time is roughly:

1. **Horde** or a bot match, to get used to the swing and the parry with
   nothing at stake.
2. **Free for all**, for volume of fights.
3. **Frontline**, once dying constantly no longer tells you anything.
4. **Duel servers**, when you want to fix something specific.

Skipping straight to Frontline is what most people do, and it is why most
people spend their first ten hours convinced the combat is random.

## Running these on your own server

Server map names are the mode prefix plus the map name, so `FFA_Grad` is Grad
in free for all. The [dedicated server guide](/en/dedicated-server-guide/) has
the map rotation setup and the full prefix list.
