# Weapons and Loadouts

Mordhau gives you a fixed budget of points and lets you spend it across a
weapon, armour and perks. Everything competes for the same pool, so a loadout
is a series of trades rather than a shopping list: heavier armour means a
cheaper weapon, more perks mean less armour.

This page is about how to think about that trade. What each individual weapon
does in numbers is a separate job, and one this page does not yet do — see
[what this page still needs](#what-this-page-still-needs) at the bottom.

## The families

**One-handed.** Shorter reach, faster windups, and they free your other hand
for a shield. Fast weapons are harder to read but also harder to
[drag](/en/combat-mechanics/) usefully, because there is less animation to
stretch.

**Two-handed.** The middle of the game. Enough reach to contest most fights,
enough windup length that accels and drags actually do something, and enough
damage that trades go your way.

**Polearms.** Reach is the whole argument. You win by standing where they
cannot reach you and punishing them for closing. In a corridor or a crowd that
is decisive; against someone who gets inside your range it is miserable.

**Ranged.** Bows and crossbows. A different game from the melee entirely, and
the one most likely to make you unpopular on a Frontline server.

**Shields.** Trade offensive options for a much larger defensive window. They
change how you are approached more than how you fight.

**Throwables and tools.** Firebombs, throwing weapons, and the toolbox, which
builds spikes and repairs objectives and is common on Frontline.

## Reach is the first thing to look at

The [combat mechanics](/en/combat-mechanics/) page makes the point that
standing at the edge of their range while inside your own is worth more than
any feint. Which weapon you carry decides where that edge is.

A longer weapon does not simply beat a shorter one. It wins at the distance it
was built for and loses inside that distance, because the same length that
keeps someone away is the length that gets caught on walls, teammates and their
body when they step in. Picking a weapon is largely picking which distance you
intend to fight at, and then actually fighting there.

## Speed changes what you can do, not just how fast you do it

Windup length is the raw material for accels and drags. A slow weapon has a
long windup, which is more animation to manipulate, so its swings can be made
to land much earlier or much later than they look. A fast weapon has less to
work with, and wins instead by simply arriving before the parry does.

That is why two players with very different weapons can both be doing something
sensible, and why copying a loadout without copying the playstyle it was built
for usually goes badly.

## Armour is bought from the same pocket

Armour is spent in tiers, and every point that goes into it is a point not
going into your weapon or your perks. Heavier armour absorbs more but costs
points and movement.

::: warning
Damage in Mordhau is not a single number: weapons deal different kinds of
damage and armour resists them differently, which is why a weapon that shreds
an unarmoured opponent can feel useless against a heavily armoured one. The
specific interactions are worth checking in game against the build you actually
run, since patches adjust them.
:::

## Building a loadout that works

1. **Pick the distance you want to fight at**, then the weapon that owns it.
2. **Spend what is left on armour**, not the other way round. A loadout built
   armour first tends to leave you with a weapon you do not enjoy.
3. **Take perks last**, and only ones you will notice. A perk that saves you
   half a second once a match cost you armour for the whole match.
4. **Run it for a few hours before judging it.** Most loadouts feel wrong for
   the first hour because they are asking you to fight differently.

If you are new, take something two-handed with medium armour and leave the
optimisation alone until the combat itself stops being the hard part.

## What this page still needs

This page covers how to choose. It does not yet carry the numbers: per-weapon
damage, windup and release timings, point costs, or armour values.

Those need someone who can test them in game and record what they actually
measure, because they shift with patches and a confidently wrong table is worse
than no table. If you have run these tests, that is a genuinely valuable
contribution — [how to contribute](/en/contributing/) covers how to send it,
and the [accuracy rules](/en/contributing/terms-rules) cover marking anything
you could not verify.
